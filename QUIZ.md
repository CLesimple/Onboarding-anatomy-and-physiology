# Chapter Quiz Questions — Anatomy & Physiology of Hearing

Three questions after each chapter: 🟢 Easy · 🟡 Medium · 🔴 Hard.
Answers are given in *italics* under each question.

---

## Chapter 0 — Hearing by the numbers

🟢 **Easy:** How many bones (ossicles) are in the middle ear?
*Three — malleus, incus, stapes (the smallest bones in the body).*

🟡 **Medium:** Roughly what is the dynamic range of hearing, from softest to loudest, in terms of pressure?
*About 10^6 in pressure (~120 dB).*

🔴 **Hard:** What phenomenon lets the ear *emit* sound, and what is it used for clinically?
*Otoacoustic emissions (OAEs), produced by the active cochlear amplifier (outer hair cells). Used for newborn hearing screening.*

## Chapter 1 — Why we need hearing

🟢 **Easy:** Name two properties of hearing that vision lacks.
*Any two of: omnidirectional, always-on, no line-of-sight required (works in the dark / around corners / during sleep).*

🟡 **Medium:** Why do we say the mammalian and avian middle ear are examples of convergent evolution?
*They evolved independently, from different ancestral jaw/skull bones — hearing did not evolve "from birds."*

🔴 **Hard:** Why are sounds (rather than lights) typically used for alarms?
*Hearing is omnidirectional, needs no line-of-sight, works during sleep, and is the fastest sense (~8–10 ms reaction).*

## Chapter 2 — From sound to the brain

🟢 **Easy:** What is the audible frequency range for humans?
*Approximately 20 Hz – 20 kHz.*

🟡 **Medium:** Name the two paths by which mechanical stimuli reach the cochlea.
*Air conduction (the normal path) and bone conduction (through the skull).*

🔴 **Hard:** Why is the dB SPL scale logarithmic rather than linear?
*The dynamic range is enormous (~10^6 in pressure); a logarithmic scale compresses it into manageable numbers and better matches perception.*

---

## Chapter 3 — Anatomy & physiology of the ear

🟢 **Easy:** Which part of the ear performs impedance matching?
*The middle ear (tympanic membrane + ossicles).*

🟡 **Medium:** Along the basilar membrane, where are high vs low frequencies detected?
*Base = high frequencies, apex = low frequencies (tonotopy).*

🔴 **Hard:** What is the functional difference between inner and outer hair cells?
*Inner hair cells are the true sensory transducers (carry most afferent fibers to the brain); outer hair cells provide motility and act as the cochlear amplifier.*

---

## Chapter 4 — Main pathologies

🟢 **Easy:** What are the two main categories of hearing loss by lesion site?
*Conductive (transmission) and sensorineural (perception). (Plus central/retrocochlear.)*

🟡 **Medium:** Otosclerosis affects which structure, and what type of hearing loss does it cause?
*Fixation of the stapes; it causes a conductive hearing loss.*

🔴 **Hard:** A patient has a sensorineural-type loss but a normal cochlea — where might the lesion be?
*Retrocochlear / central — e.g. an acoustic neuroma (vestibular schwannoma) or a lesion along the central auditory pathway.*
